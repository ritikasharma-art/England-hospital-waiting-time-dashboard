What you can do while you wait

  • Check your referral is in place. Contact the hospital or your GP if you haven’t heard anything.
  • Look up other hospitals. You can usually choose where to be treated. My Planned Care shows waits by hospital.
  • Know your rights. The NHS Constitution gives you the right to start consultant-led treatment within 18 weeks. If that can’t happen, your local NHS should try to offer an alternative. Search “NHS waiting times rights” on nhs.uk.
  • Tell someone if you get worse. New or worsening symptoms matter, so contact your GP or NHS 111.

What the terms mean

  • Waiting under 18 weeks: of everyone currently on a waiting list, the share whose wait so far is under 18 weeks. This is the measure behind the standard.
  • 18-week standard: the NHS Constitution standard is that 92% of people waiting should have waited under 18 weeks. NHS England’s planning goals set lower interim national targets on the way (for 2026/27, 70%).
  • Median wait: half of people have waited less than this.
  • 92nd percentile: 92 in 100 people have waited less than this.
  • Pathway: one referral. Some people have more than one, so counts of pathways are a little higher than counts of people.
  • Per 100,000 people: the number of waiting pathways divided by the area’s resident population, times 100,000. It lets large and small areas be compared fairly. It reflects how many people need care as well as how well the local NHS is coping, so a higher rate is not always a worse service.
  • Over a year: waiting more than 52 weeks.
Data notes and sources

Waiting times: NHS England, Consultant-led Referral to Treatment waiting times, monthly full extracts January to September 2026, and the England time series to September 2026. Totals are reported pathways without estimates for trusts that did not submit data, so they can be a little lower than NHS England’s headline figures. Areas are the commissioning ICB. In April 2026 some ICBs merged (42 areas became 36), so the trend for a merged area starts in April, while England totals cover all areas throughout. Percentages for a type of care are not shown in the ranking or map where an area has fewer than 100 people waiting, because small numbers swing widely. Where an area’s total changes by more than 15% from one month to the next, a warning is shown, because sudden jumps like this can come from a trust’s data being missing or moving between areas rather than from real change.

Population and rates: Office for National Statistics (ONS) mid-2024 population estimates for 2021 Lower layer Super Output Areas, published 7 November 2025, added up to the 36 ICBs using the ONS April 2026 lookup (all 33,755 areas in England matched; total 58.6 million). Waiting data are from 2026 and population is from mid-2024, so rates are approximate. ONS asks that populations are rounded to the nearest 100 when quoted, so that is how they are shown here. Rates over 1,000 are rounded to the nearest 10. Rates divide all waiting pathways by all residents of every age.

Map: ONS Integrated Care Boards (April 2026) boundaries, generalised, redrawn in simplified form so small coastlines and islands are approximate.
